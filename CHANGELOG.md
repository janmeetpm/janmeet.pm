# Changelog

Reverse-chronological. Every entry is dated `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM IST` and summarises a change
to the site or its tooling, in enough detail that the *reason* survives — the commit log
records what moved, this records why it moved.

Not part of the deploy. Update it in the same change that touches `index.html`, `DESIGN.md`
or `tools/`; add the entry under **Unreleased** while the work sits in the working tree and
move it under a dated heading when it lands on `main`.

## Unreleased

### 2026-08-19 19:40 — two design experiments in `experiments/` (not committed, not deployed)

`02-apple-fluid` and `03-emil-craft`, both generated by a `patch.py` that reads the real
`index.html` and applies named exact-match replacements — so they re-base onto the live file
with one command instead of drifting from it, and the patch list reads as a diff. Nothing here
touches the site; `experiments/` is gitignored and not part of the deploy.

- **02** moves the phone folds and the drawer to springs, because a CSS transition
  interpolates from where it was told to start and cannot be grabbed mid-flight. Adds a drag
  handle on the `+`/`−` box (1:1, rubber-banded, released on projected momentum), swipe-to-
  dismiss on the drawer, Apple's full tap contract, size-specific tracking on `h1`/`.lead`, a
  scroll edge under the top bar, and the `prefers-reduced-transparency` /
  `prefers-contrast: more` blocks the site was missing entirely.
- **03** clips the nav's active state instead of cross-fading two ambers, adds a copy control
  to the email with a 2px sweep as the confirmation, reveals entries on scroll, and stops
  animating folds that repeat inside 450ms.

Recorded here because two findings apply to the real file regardless of whether either
variant ports:

- **The live site has no phone navigation with scripting off.** `nav.cmds` is `position:fixed`
  and `clip-path`-hidden below 620px, and only `body.navopen` — added by JS — reveals it. Both
  variants gate the drawer behind an `html.js` class for this reason. Worth fixing on its own.
- **A spring's settle thresholds must scale to the value being animated.** One shared absolute
  epsilon stopped the drawer 22.4% short of open, which reads as a snap. Measured, not
  reasoned about. Relatedly: spring overshoot is set by the damping ratio, not by how hard the
  flick was — at damping .8 a 600px/s and a 2600px/s release overshoot identically.

`experiments/README.md` carries the full contract for both, including what each skill
recommends that DESIGN.md forbids (chiefly `backdrop-filter` and `filter: blur()`), the costs
to decide before porting, and what could not be verified: no spring was ever watched at full
size, because the automation browser runs the page in a hidden tab where `requestAnimationFrame`
is starved. The timings were measured against a synthetic clock instead.

## 2026-08-20

### 2026-08-20 — page one rewritten in plain language (owner direction)

Owner's direction: page one has to be understood by a reader without deep technical
knowledge, tell the story of how inquisitive he is and how much he owned inside the
conversational agent, and the north star is to sell him. Everything above `02 systems` was
written for an AI engineer, which is not who opens the page first — a recruiter or a founder
does, and they were being asked to decode *adherence*, *orchestration*, *eval coverage* and
*inference economics* before reaching a single reason to care.

The four numbers survive as the site's big idea, but page one now states them as four plain
questions and section 02 onward keeps the technical vocabulary. Non-technical reader gets the
argument; technical reader gets the terms one scroll later. Nothing was dumbed down, and no
claim was added that is not already supported elsewhere in the file.

- **h1** — was "ai pm building enterprise agent platforms that survive production": a category
  label in insider words. Now "i make ai assistants reliable enough to put in front of real
  customers". First person, plain, and it is a promise rather than a job description.
- **spec `owns`** — was `adherence · latency · cost/interaction · eval coverage`. Now `does it
  follow the rules · is it fast · what it costs · how well it's tested`. Same four things,
  legible cold. Verified all rows still render on one line at desktop width.
- **spec `surface` → `works on`** — the label was jargon and the value was five nouns. Now
  names the things themselves: chat agents, voice agents, the models behind them, the tests.
- **spec `currently`** — dropped "enterprise agents" for "the ai agents", consistent with the
  rest of page one.
- **about** — was one 130-word block of nouns. Now two turns, because the owner asked for a
  story and the second question is the story: *how do you know which part is broken?* First
  turn is ownership, and it names the modules concretely — the piece that decides the next
  step, the piece that looks up the company's policies, the piece that picks the model and its
  cost, the tests that catch it first. Second turn is the curiosity: reading transcripts turn
  by turn until the dropped step has a name, and the three things that came out of that habit
  (built the eval harness, moved the knowledge layer into a screen the support team edits
  itself, builds the prototype rather than specs it). Every one of those is already documented
  in SYS-01, SYS-04 and the stack `build` row — no new claim.
- **meter** — heading was "every agent turn has to fit inside this", which assumes the reader
  knows what a turn is. Now "what has to happen before the customer hears an answer". The
  caption states the budget as "roughly one second" (text 950ms, voice 1050ms in `DATA`) and
  explains why voice pays twice, instead of asserting it.
- **note** — "whoever is paying for the tokens" became "whoever is paying the bill".
- **`<title>`, `meta description`, `og:description`** — rewritten to match, since an unfurl is
  page one for anyone who meets the site through a link.

Not changed: `02 systems` through `07 contact` keep their vocabulary. That layering is the
point — page one sells, the rest proves.

### 2026-08-19 — interaction polish (uncommitted at session start, author not this session)

Present in the working tree before the copy pass; recorded here for completeness, exact time
unknown.

- Every `:hover` rule on an interactive surface moved behind `@media (hover:hover) and
  (pointer:fine)`. Touch fires `:hover` on press and leaves it on the last-tapped element,
  which read as a second active state fighting `.on`.
- Shared press feedback: one `--press:scale(.985)` transform on nav, fold rules, contact rows,
  suggestion chips, the theme button and the hamburger, so every tap confirms the same way.
- Two easing tokens (`--ease-out`, `--ease-drawer`) replace inline cubic-beziers.
- Phone nav drawer wipes down via `clip-path` instead of appearing on a `display` toggle —
  a translate would show through the 90%-opaque chrome it sits above.
- Section folds close faster than they open (.22s vs .3s), on the reasoning that opening is
  the reader waiting on content and closing is the page answering.
- ASCII meter: the type-in reveal is now one-shot and gated on an `IntersectionObserver`, so a
  `?tab=contact` deep link no longer types it out offscreen; every other path into `render()`
  (modality switch, width re-fit, re-opening the about fold) paints the finished state.
  `prefers-reduced-motion` is now watched for mid-session changes instead of read once.
